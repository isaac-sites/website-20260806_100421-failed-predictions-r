## Introduction

Before [Dorothy Martin]({{ 'dorothy-martin/' | relative_url }})'s [automatic-writing]({{ 'source-test/' | relative_url }}) messages identified the planet Clarion as the source of her flood prophecy, the name had already entered UFO contactee culture through the claims of Truman Bethurum. This earlier history matters because it shows that "Clarion" was not a unique or previously unknown element revealed through Martin's writings. Instead, it was already circulating publicly within the growing flying-saucer movement of the early 1950s. That does not prove Martin consciously borrowed the idea from Bethurum, but it does demonstrate that her claimed extraterrestrial source emerged within an existing body of UFO folklore rather than in isolation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

## Was Clarion Already Part of UFO Folklore?

By the time Martin's predictions attracted national attention in late 1954, Truman Bethurum had already become one of the best-known American UFO contactees. He claimed that his encounters began in 1952 while working on a road construction project in the Nevada desert. According to his account, he repeatedly boarded a flying saucer commanded by a female captain named Aura Rhanes, who said she came from the planet Clarion. Bethurum first related these stories at flying-saucer gatherings before publishing them more widely in his 1954 book *Aboard a Flying Saucer*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

Because Bethurum lectured extensively and participated in well-known contactee conventions such as the Giant Rock gatherings, the name "Clarion" entered the vocabulary of the UFO movement before Martin's prophecy became famous. Within contactee circles, Clarion was therefore already recognised as the supposed home of advanced "Space Brothers" rather than a completely novel [revelation]({{ 'revelation/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Bethurum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Bethurum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Bethurum</span></span></span>

## Bethurum's Earlier Clarion Claims

Bethurum described Clarion as an inhabited world within the Solar System whose existence had escaped scientific discovery because it remained permanently hidden from Earth. Different versions of his story placed it behind the Moon or otherwise positioned so that it could never be directly observed from Earth. The inhabitants were portrayed as physically human, morally advanced and technologically superior, with spacecraft capable of effortless interplanetary travel. They warned humanity about war, especially the dangers posed by nuclear weapons, themes that became common across many contactee narratives of the period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

These geographical claims were criticised almost immediately. Astronomers and many UFO investigators pointed out that a planet of substantial size could not remain permanently concealed in the manner Bethurum proposed. Its gravitational effects and orbital behaviour would make such a world detectable even if it were visually obscured. As a result, the hidden-planet explanation became one of the most disputed aspects of his account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

## The Hidden-Planet Idea in Contactee Culture

Bethurum's Clarion should be understood within the broader culture of 1950s contactee beliefs rather than as an isolated invention. Numerous contactees claimed personal meetings with benevolent extraterrestrials who delivered spiritual teachings alongside warnings about humanity's future. These narratives blended science-fiction imagery, religious concepts and occult ideas into a single worldview in which advanced civilisations acted as guardians of Earth's moral development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flying saucer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flying saucer</span></span></span>

Within this environment, hidden planets served an important narrative purpose. They explained why benevolent extraterrestrial civilisations could exist without being confirmed by conventional astronomy while allowing believers to reconcile extraordinary claims with the absence of observable evidence. Clarion therefore functioned less as an astronomical hypothesis than as a symbolic location within contactee mythology.

Martin's own portrayal of Clarion differed in significant respects. In *When Prophecy Fails*, Clarion appears less as a conventional physical planet visited by spacecraft crews and more as the home of spiritually advanced intelligences communicating through automatic writing. Even so, the shared name linked her movement to an already established strand of contactee tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

## What Similarity Can—and Cannot—Prove

The overlap between Bethurum's and Martin's use of Clarion establishes an important historical fact: the name was already publicly available before Martin's flood prophecy. It weakens any suggestion that the appearance of "Clarion" in her automatic-writing sessions demonstrates access to uniquely unknown information. Instead, it shows that the concept already existed within the cultural environment from which many flying-saucer beliefs emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum, Truman (1898-1969) &#124; Encyclopedia.com...</span></span></span>

At the same time, the similarity alone does not establish direct copying. There is no known document in which Martin acknowledged taking the name from Bethurum, nor is there definitive evidence tracing a direct line of influence between the two individuals. Historians therefore distinguish between **shared cultural context** and **demonstrated borrowing**. The first is well supported by the chronology; the second remains unproven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

## Why the Earlier Clarion Matters for Assessing Martin's Prophecy

For the study of failed UFO predictions, Bethurum's earlier Clarion narrative provides valuable context. Martin's automatic-writing messages were presented as communications from extraterrestrial intelligences, yet one of their central identifying features—the planet Clarion—was already part of the public mythology of the contactee movement. This does not resolve questions about Martin's personal [sincerity]({{ 'sincerity/' | relative_url }}), but it does show that the symbolic framework of her revelations drew upon ideas already circulating among UFO believers.

Seen in this historical sequence, the connection between Bethurum and Martin illustrates how contactee traditions could recycle and reshape existing motifs.   function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {

      });
