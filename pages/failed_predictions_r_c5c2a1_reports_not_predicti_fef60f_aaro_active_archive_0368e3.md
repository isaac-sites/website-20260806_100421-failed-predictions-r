## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) uses an **active archive** to manage [unresolved reports]({{ 'unresolved-reports/' | relative_url }}) that lack enough reliable information for a full technical assessment. This archive is a records-management and analytical tool, not a holding area for confirmed mysteries or suspected extraterrestrial events. Understanding that distinction is essential when discussing [failed predictions relating to UFOs]({{ 'failed-predictions-relating-to-ufos/' | relative_url }}). A report placed in the active archive documents an event that cannot currently be explained because of insufficient evidence, not because investigators have concluded that it involved extraordinary technology. AARO's reporting process deliberately separates incomplete cases from those that contain enough data to justify deeper specialist analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Reports Enter the Active Archive

AARO defines the active archive as a repository for reports that cannot undergo comprehensive analysis because the available information is incomplete or inadequate. Rather than forcing a conclusion from weak evidence, investigators retain these cases so they remain available if additional information later emerges.

Common reasons for placement in the archive include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Missing or incomplete sensor data.
* Insufficient metadata, such as time, location or instrument settings.
* A lack of corroborating evidence from radar, other sensors or additional observers.
* Reports submitted long after the event, making reconstruction difficult.
* Image or video quality too poor to support confident identification.

</div>

This reflects a broader scientific principle also highlighted by NASA's independent UAP study: many reported events cannot be conclusively characterised because the available observations lack calibration, multiple measurements or sufficient contextual information. Poor-quality data limit what investigators can determine, regardless of how unusual a report may initially appear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report-0.pdf?emrc=6668aea5c3e03" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap independent study team final report 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## How Archived Cases Differ from Specialist Referrals

One of AARO's most important procedural distinctions is between **insufficient-data cases** and **cases selected for further technical investigation**.

A report in the active archive has **not** been judged especially anomalous. Instead, investigators conclude that there is too little reliable information to perform a meaningful analysis. The case remains stored for future reference but is not treated as evidence supporting any particular explanation.

By contrast, a small number of reports are referred for additional specialist analysis because they contain enough observational data to justify further examination. Referral therefore reflects **data quality and analytical potential**, not confirmation that an event involved advanced or unknown technology.

This distinction helps prevent an important misunderstanding. An archived report is unresolved because investigators cannot responsibly reach a conclusion. A specialist referral is unresolved because investigators have enough information to warrant additional technical work. These are different administrative outcomes serving different investigative purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/space/pentagon-ufo-report-says-most-sightings-ordinary-objects-phenomena-2024-03-08/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Most sightings were identified as ordinary objects or phenomena. ## What the Unresolved Totals Do and Do Not Show

The size of the active archive is sometimes presented in public discussion as though it represents a growing inventory of unexplained craft. AARO's reporting framework does not support that interpretation.

Recent annual reports illustrate the pattern:

* In Fiscal Year 2024, AARO considered 757 reports. Of these, 444 entered the active archive because the available data were insufficient, while only 21 were selected for further analysis. Hundreds of other reports were resolved as ordinary objects such as balloons, birds, drones, satellites and aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/aaro-fy24-consolidated-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[newparadigminstitute.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro fy24 consolidated report – New Paradigm Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2024</span></span></span>
* In Fiscal Year 2025, AARO reported that 191 of 319 newly received reports were placed in the active archive for insufficient data. Only nine were referred for additional analysis, while many others were identified as conventional objects including balloons, birds, aircraft, satellites, drones, a rocket launch and even a jet pack.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/uap/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science UAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceUAP - NASA ScienceFebruary 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 23, 2026</span></span></span>

These figures demonstrate that unresolved totals are largely a measure of **data limitations**, not of unexplained phenomena. Reports can remain unresolved because investigators lack enough evidence to eliminate ordinary explanations, not because extraordinary explanations have become more likely.

## Why the Archive Can Still Be Useful

Although archived reports are not suitable for definitive case analysis, they still have practical value.

Keeping incomplete reports allows AARO to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* identify recurring reporting patterns;
* compare later incidents with earlier observations;
* detect possible clusters that may justify renewed investigation;
* reopen cases if additional sensor data or witness information become available;
* improve future reporting standards by identifying common information gaps.

</div>

The archive therefore functions as a living database rather than a permanent dead end. A case's placement reflects the current state of available evidence and may change if new information emerges.

## What the Active Archive Means for Claims About Failed UFO Predictions

The existence of an active archive does not provide support for predictions about future extraterrestrial disclosure, alien visitation or other forecast events. The [archive records]({{ 'archive-records/' | relative_url }}) historical observations whose causes cannot presently be determined with confidence because of limited evidence. It does not contain [official findings]({{ 'official-findings/' | relative_url }}) that extraordinary explanations are correct, nor does it postpone judgment until a predicted event eventually occurs.

This distinction matters when evaluating failed predictions. A prediction succeeds or fails according to whether its forecast happens. An unresolved report concerns an already observed event whose available evidence remains insufficient for confident identification. Confusing these two categories turns uncertainty into apparent support for claims that have not been independently verified.

AARO's reporting framework is specifically designed to avoid that mistake by separating **incomplete evidence**, **cases worthy of further technical analysis**, and **identified ordinary phenomena**. Across its published reports, AARO has also stated that it has found no evidence of extraterrestrial beings, activity or technology in the cases it has investigated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/aaro-fy24-consolidated-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[newparadigminstitute.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">aaro fy24 consolidated report – New Paradigm Institute</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2024</span></span></span>
