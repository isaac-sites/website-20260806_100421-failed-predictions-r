## Introduction

When a dated UFO prediction fails, postponement keeps the promised event alive while abandoning the original claim’s clearest test. The spacecraft did not arrive on Tuesday, but believers are told that it will arrive later: preparations took longer than expected, humanity was [not ready]({{ 'not-ready/' | relative_url }}), hostile forces interfered, or the date was only provisional. The event remains physical and dramatic, yet its deadline becomes increasingly flexible.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/failed_predictions_r_c5c2a1_postponed_ufo_deadli_e948ae-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Postponement" width="1024" height="559" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

This mechanism is especially visible in repeated alien-landing claims. A near date creates urgency, publicity and practical commitment; once it passes, a distant replacement date restores hope and gives the movement time to rebuild its explanation. The crucial change is therefore not simply from one calendar entry to another. Responsibility for the failure is moved away from the predictor, while the next deadline is usually made less vulnerable to immediate checking.

Postponement does not prove that every revision is dishonest. Forecasts can legitimately change when new evidence appears. But repeated UFO deadlines deserve suspicion when the original date was presented as revealed knowledge, the revision follows only after failure, and no independent evidence explains why the supposedly advanced source gave the wrong timetable.

## Common forms of date shifting

Failed UFO dates are not all postponed in the same way. Some are replaced immediately by a new day; others dissolve into a year, an era or an indefinite period of “imminence”. Each method protects the central promise by weakening its exposure to another decisive failure.

The sequence illustrates how a missed appointment can be treated not as disconfirmation but as an incomplete stage in an unfolding schedule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A recently published book, Aboard a Flying Saucer by UFO contactee Truman Bethurum, had described his alleged interactions with beings fr...</span></span></span>

That case must now be used carefully. Thomas Kelly’s recent archival research argues that *When Prophecy Fails*, the classic account of Martin’s followers, distorted the group’s behaviour, understated its earlier publicity activity and exaggerated the idea that the members collectively strengthened their commitment after failure. The missed dates remain documented, but the episode cannot safely support a universal claim that disappointed believers always respond in the same psychological way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1002%2Fjhbs.70043" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">But When Prophecy Fails (1956), the now-canonical account of the event, claimed the opposite: that the group doubled down on its beliefs...</span></span></span>

**The long reset** replaces a failed near-term date with one years or decades away. This preserves [specificity]({{ 'specificity/' | relative_url }})—the event still has a date—but removes the immediate pressure created by the original prediction. The new horizon may also be linked to a symbolic calendar boundary, such as the beginning of a century or a supposed planetary cycle, making the change appear meaningful rather than improvised.

**The conditional delay** says the date depended on human behaviour. Humanity may be too violent, insufficiently enlightened or unprepared for contact. Because readiness has no agreed measurement, the claim becomes difficult to test: any continued absence can be attributed to continuing moral or spiritual inadequacy.

**The external-[interference]({{ 'interference/' | relative_url }}) delay** blames governments, hostile extraterrestrials, negative energies, warfare or world events. This retains the idea that the prediction was authentic while claiming that circumstances changed after it was issued. It is persuasive within a closed narrative because every disruption can be absorbed as further evidence of a cosmic conflict.

**The rolling window** replaces “on this date” with “during this period”, “before the end of the cycle” or simply “soon”. The promised event remains close enough to sustain expectation but no longer has a single moment at which failure must be acknowledged. Sociological studies of disappointed prophecy describe this broader practice as rationalisation: unfulfilled elements are relocated into the future while the earlier failure is retrospectively reinterpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/1999-dawson.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[Gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails and Faith Persists</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 8, 2013 — 5 May 2004 — “the denial of failure is not just another option, but the common mode of adaptation of millennial groups foll...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2013</span></span></span>

These forms can be combined. A prediction may move first by a few months, then by several years, and finally into an indefinite future dependent on humanity’s spiritual development. The result is not merely a revised timetable but a gradual retreat from [falsifiability]({{ 'falsifiability/' | relative_url }}).

## Unarius and the repeatedly delayed landing

The Unarius Academy of Science provides the clearest sustained example because its expected extraterrestrial landing survived several missed deadlines and remained part of the movement’s public life for decades.

[Ruth Norman]({{ 'ruth-norman/' | relative_url }}), known within Unarius as Uriel, announced in 1974 that a fleet representing an interplanetary confederation would land on Earth. The group acquired a 67-acre property near Jamul, California, as a landing site and prepared for the arrival of the “Space Brothers”. The expected event was unusually concrete: spacecraft, extraterrestrial occupants, a physical location and a transformative public encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/7811/chapter-abstract/152993807" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet"># 9 Preparation for the Landing... The first timetable moved quickly. A landing expected before the end of 1974 was shifted to September 1975, with the delay attributed to continuing efforts to prepare humanity. When September passed without a landing, Norman explained that trauma from an alleged previous life had affected the prediction. The error was therefore assigned not to the extraterrestrial source or to the wider doctrine, but to a personal psychic obstruction that could be addressed through the group’s therapeutic practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Norman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ruth Norman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ruth Norman</span></span></span>

This response did more than excuse a mistaken date. It folded the failure into Unarius’s existing system of past-life narratives. The missed landing became evidence for the need to uncover and heal ancient trauma, giving the disappointment a new function inside the movement. Diana Tumminia’s long-term study of Unarius describes this broader process as interpretive adaptation and continuing narrative invention rather than a simple, one-off excuse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/3712078" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on jstor.org.</span></span></span>

Norman then made another highly testable commitment. In 1976 she reportedly placed a wager with Ladbrokes that extraterrestrials would land within a year. After that period also passed, the expected arrival was eventually relocated to 2001, a date far enough away to restore anticipation without requiring an immediate reckoning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Norman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ruth Norman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ruth Norman</span></span></span>

By the 1980s and 1990s, 2001 had become the prominent public deadline. Contemporary newspaper reports described a fleet of 33 spacecraft, carrying thousands of extraterrestrials, expected to land at or near the Unarius property. Preparations, ceremonies and media attention helped turn the future landing into a recurring element of group identity rather than a single prediction awaiting confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-09-29-vw-10514-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latimes.com">[Los Angeles Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on latimes.com.</span></span></span>

The year 2001 passed without the fleet. The failure did not produce another comparably firm public date. Instead, the explanation became more conditional: representatives said that humanity was not ready or that earlier statements had been misunderstood. An account published after the missed deadline noted that followers still regarded the landing as imminent, despite the spacecraft’s failure to arrive in 2001.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2003-jan-12-me-then12-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latimes.com">[Los Angeles Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Los Angeles Times Flying Saucer Society Sought Peak Experience on Baldy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Los Angeles Times Flying Saucer Society Sought Peak Experience on Baldy</span></span></span>

The progression is revealing:

1. **A near, physical prediction:** spacecraft will land within months.
2. **A revised near date:** additional preparation is needed.
3. **A personal explanation:** psychic trauma distorted the timing.
4. **A distant deadline:** the fleet will arrive in 2001.
5. **An open-ended condition:** the landing awaits [human readiness]({{ 'human-readiness/' | relative_url }}).

At each stage, the physical arrival remained possible in principle. What steadily disappeared was the original claim’s sharp temporal boundary.

## From a fixed landing to a spiritual process

Some postponed UFO predictions eventually cease to be mainly about dates. The expected arrival is reframed as the end point of a gradual spiritual transformation, making the calendar secondary to believers’ conduct.

This shift appeared in online UFO movements during the 1990s. The Ground Crew Project, associated with Sheldan Nidle, reportedly anticipated a mass landing of millions of spacecraft by December 1996 as Earth passed through a supposed “photon belt”. After the predicted events of 1996–97 failed to occur, the narrative increasingly stressed humanity’s spiritual work and preparation rather than a single, externally delivered technological transformation. Scholars Susan Palmer and Christopher Helland characterised the movement’s response as a set of narrative techniques for managing prophetic failure; later summaries describe the promised physical intervention being reconceived as a process requiring human participation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Crew_Project" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

This is more durable than simply announcing another date. A spacecraft landing can be checked at a specified place and time. An improvement in planetary consciousness cannot be measured in the same straightforward way. Believers can always be told that meaningful progress is occurring invisibly, that their activism is accelerating contact, or that further preparation remains necessary.

The shift also changes the follower’s role. Before failure, the believer waits for superior beings to act. Afterwards, the believer may be asked to meditate, spread teachings, establish local groups or raise collective consciousness. Continued participation then becomes part of the condition for fulfilment. The absence of spacecraft no longer tests the prophecy independently because the outcome is said to depend on an unfinished human task.

Not every movement follows this exact path, and the available membership evidence for early internet groups is often weak. Helland noted that the Ground Crew’s online character made it difficult to establish how many followers remained after its [failed predictions]({{ 'failed-predictions-relating-to-ufos/' | relative_url }}). That uncertainty is itself important: an enduring website or teaching does not show that all original believers accepted the postponement. Some may simply leave without producing a visible record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Crew_Project" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Why postponement works

Postponement succeeds because it preserves several valuable parts of the original claim at once.

First, it protects the **promised outcome**. The expected landing, rescue or public contact is not withdrawn; it is transferred to a future in which it can still organise hope.

Second, it protects the **authority of the source**. The extraterrestrial communicator may remain infallible because the human channel misunderstood the message, conditions changed, or opponents intervened. The failed date becomes a transmission problem rather than evidence against the alleged contact.

Third, it protects **past commitment**. People who have invested money, reputation or years of participation do not have to conclude that the entire project was mistaken. A revised timetable allows preparations to be described as early rather than wasted.

Fourth, it creates **new explanatory material**. The failure can generate teachings about patience, spiritual readiness, cosmic opposition or hidden stages of contact. Tumminia’s work on Unarius is particularly useful here because it shows prophecy being sustained through a continuously elaborated communal story, not merely through a private moment of denial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/book/7811/chapter/152989636" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Academic2 How Prophecy Never Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Disconfirmation. Reaffirmation Reaffirmation. A New Date A New Date. Expand... Prophecy Never Fails: Myth and Reality in a Flying-Saucer...</span></span></span>

However, postponement does not guarantee survival. Members may doubt the explanation, withdraw or leave the movement. In the Unarius case, some followers reportedly rejected Norman’s account of the failed 1975 landing and departed. Recent archival work on Dorothy Martin’s group similarly warns against assuming that every public rationalisation represents the private response of every participant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Norman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ruth Norman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ruth Norman</span></span></span>

The mechanism therefore works selectively. It gives committed participants a route to continue, but it may also expose divisions between those willing to accept a revised narrative and those who regard the missed deadline as decisive.

## Revision or evasion?

A changed prediction is not automatically evasive. Scientific and practical forecasts are routinely revised because new observations alter the underlying estimate. The difference lies in how the revision treats evidence, error and accountability.

A credible revision usually has several features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The change is announced **before** the original deadline where possible.
* The forecaster identifies specific new evidence that altered the estimate.
* The method that produced the original date is explained.
* Responsibility for the error is accepted rather than transferred to unmeasurable forces.
* The revised claim remains testable.
* Repeated failures reduce confidence in the source.

</div>

An evasive postponement tends to show the opposite pattern. It appears only after the deadline has passed; relies on explanations that outsiders cannot check; preserves the predictor’s authority unchanged; and makes the next prediction broader or more conditional. “The fleet was delayed by a documented astronomical event” would at least invite investigation. “Humanity’s vibrations remain unsuitable” provides no agreed observation that could confirm or refute the claim.

Another warning sign is **asymmetric interpretation**. Anything unusual before the deadline—a light in the sky, a coincidence, a dream—is treated as confirmation, while the complete absence of the promised landing is not treated as disconfirmation. Evidence is then being judged by different standards depending on whether it supports the belief.

A final test is to preserve the original wording. Before accepting a revised date, readers should record what was promised, where it was supposed to happen, who issued it and what explanation was given in advance. Later interpretations can then be compared with the initial claim. Without that record, a precise prediction can quietly become a symbolic expectation, and a failed deadline can disappear from the movement’s retelling.

## The real cost of moving the date

The most important loss in postponement is not calendrical accuracy but accountability. A firm UFO date gives the public a rare opportunity to test an extraordinary claim. When that date is replaced after failure without an evidence-based explanation, the new prediction no longer carries the same evidential weight.

Repeated postponement also creates a misleading sense that the prophecy has never quite failed. Each individual deadline is treated as an adjustment within one continuing process, although the process exists largely because its earlier promised events did not occur. In the Unarius example, the movement from 1974 to 1975, then to a one-year wager, then to 2001 and finally to conditional imminence did not strengthen the evidence for an alien fleet. It progressively reduced the precision of the claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Norman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ruth Norman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ruth Norman</span></span></span>

The fairest assessment is therefore cumulative. A new date should not erase the old one. Every missed deadline remains part of the source’s forecasting record, and confidence should decline unless the revision rests on independently verifiable evidence. Postponement may preserve the story of future contact, but it cannot preserve the original prediction’s credibility simply by moving it beyond the next page of the calendar.

    return encodeURIComponent(String(value || '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim()).replace(/%20/g, '+');
  }
  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
