## Introduction

The fact that the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) leaves some unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP) reports open does not mean it has found evidence of alien technology. In many cases, the opposite is true: investigators conclude that the available imagery shows nothing especially unusual, yet the evidence is still too limited to support a defensible identification. Rather than guessing, AARO keeps such reports in an unresolved category until additional information becomes available. This distinction is important when assessing claims that official UAP investigations somehow validate [failed predictions]({{ 'failed-predictions-relating-to-ufos/' | relative_url }}) of alien arrival. An unresolved case reflects the limits of the available evidence, not proof of an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

## What AARO Means by an Unresolved Case

AARO uses "unresolved" as an evidential classification rather than a statement about what an object is. A report can remain open because analysts cannot confidently identify the object from the available data, even when they see no signs of extraordinary technology.

The office's published imagery illustrates several versions of this distinction:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Some videos are too brief or lack supporting sensor information to identify the object confidently.
* Some recordings show what appears to be a genuine physical object, but its appearance, movement and behaviour are entirely ordinary.
* Some thermal signatures cannot be distinguished reliably from environmental effects or sensor artefacts.

</div>

In each situation, AARO prefers to acknowledge uncertainty instead of assigning an identification that cannot be supported analytically. This reflects an intelligence standard of evidence rather than a presumption that something exotic has been observed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

## How Short Videos and Missing Data Prevent Identification

Many [unresolved cases]({{ 'unresolved-cases/' | relative_url }}) stem from missing contextual information rather than mysterious flight characteristics.

AARO repeatedly notes that analysts often receive only a fragment of the information that would normally allow identification. Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Very short recordings.** Some published videos last only a few seconds or a few dozen seconds, providing too little time to establish an object's motion or behaviour reliably.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>
* **Single-sensor observations.** Infrared imagery alone may show only a heat contrast without revealing whether it is a physical object, a reflection, an atmospheric effect or a sensor artefact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>
* **Lack of corroborating telemetry.** Without radar tracks, flight information, precise range data or observations from multiple sensors, analysts cannot calculate speed, altitude or trajectory with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>
* **Missing contextual information.** Weather conditions, nearby aircraft, known balloon launches and other operational details may be unavailable, making a definitive attribution impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

</div>

These limitations explain why an object can remain unidentified even when it appears visually unremarkable. Identification requires enough information to distinguish among several plausible conventional explanations, not merely an impression that the object "looks ordinary."

## Why Ordinary Appearance Does Not Automatically Resolve a Case

Several of AARO's publicly released reports are notable precisely because they emphasise the lack of unusual characteristics.

For example, multiple unresolved European cases from 2022 and 2024 state that AARO has **high confidence** the footage depicts a physical object. However, the office also concludes that the object's "morphological features, performance characteristics, and behaviors are unremarkable and do not warrant further analysis." Even so, the precise identity cannot be established from the available evidence, so the report remains unresolved pending additional information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

This illustrates an important analytical distinction:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The object exists:** analysts are confident the sensor captured something physical.
* **The object behaves normally:** nothing in the observed motion or appearance suggests extraordinary performance.
* **The object cannot be named confidently:** insufficient evidence prevents identifying whether it was a specific balloon, aircraft, drone or another conventional object.

</div>

Leaving such a case unresolved reflects evidential discipline rather than suspicion of exotic technology.

## Why Unresolved Does Not Mean Extraordinary

One of the most common public misunderstandings is to treat every unresolved UAP report as a candidate for alien spacecraft. AARO explicitly avoids that inference.

The office's historical review found no verified evidence that any U.S. government investigation has recovered extraterrestrial technology or confirmed off-world craft. Likewise, the annual UAP reports continue to state that resolved cases overwhelmingly involve familiar objects such as balloons, birds, aircraft, drones and satellites, while unresolved cases generally lack sufficient information for a scientifically defensible conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/space/pentagon-ufo-report-says-most-sightings-ordinary-objects-phenomena-2024-03-08/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pentagon UFO report says most sightings &#x27;ordinary objects&#x27; and phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Most sightings were identified as ordinary objects or phenomena. ## Why AARO Keeps These Cases on Record

AARO does not necessarily discard ordinary-looking [unresolved reports]({{ 'unresolved-reports/' | relative_url }}). Instead, it retains them because additional evidence could eventually permit identification.

The office notes that unresolved reports contribute to historical and geographical trend analysis. If future reports from the same area, sensor or operational context provide better data, analysts may be able to revisit earlier observations and reach a firmer conclusion. Several published case summaries explicitly state that AARO will continue investigating if new information becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>

This approach reflects a broader intelligence practice: preserving incomplete cases allows later evidence to be compared across multiple observations instead of forcing premature conclusions from isolated, low-quality data.

## Why This Matters for Alien Arrival Predictions

Within the broader discussion of failed predictions about UFOs and alien arrival, AARO's unresolved ordinary-looking cases should not be interpreted as retrospective confirmation of prophetic claims.

An unresolved case means investigators cannot identify an otherwise ordinary object with sufficient confidence from limited evidence. It does not mean the object displayed extraordinary capabilities, nor does it constitute evidence of extraterrestrial origin. Consequently, the continued existence of unresolved UAP files does not reverse or validate past predictions that specific dates or events would bring public alien contact. Instead, these cases demonstrate a more cautious principle: when the evidence is inadequate, the correct conclusion is uncertainty rather than speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/Official-UAP-Imagery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[aaro.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UAP Imagery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO UAP Imagery...</span></span></span>
