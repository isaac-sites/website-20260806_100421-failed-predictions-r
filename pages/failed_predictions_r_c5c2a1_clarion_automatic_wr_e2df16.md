## Introduction

[Dorothy Martin]({{ 'dorothy-martin/' | relative_url }})’s 1954 UFO prophecy did not begin with a public sighting, an astronomical calculation or a leaked government document. It began with pages of handwriting that Martin said were produced under the direction of non-human intelligences. Through this practice, commonly called [automatic writing]({{ 'source-test/' | relative_url }}), she attributed messages to spiritually advanced beings associated with the planet Clarion. ## Martin’s claimed source on Clarion

Automatic writing is a form of claimed mediumship in which a person writes while believing that an external spirit, intelligence or force is directing the hand. Martin’s messages were therefore physical documents, but their existence demonstrated only that words had been written—not that extraterrestrial beings had supplied them. A recent archival study describes her practice as a purported means of receiving occult or psychic communications in which spirits or other forces controlled the writer’s hand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2025-kelly.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[Gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span></span></span>

According to the account later published in *When Prophecy Fails*, Martin’s claimed communications did not begin as a single, fully formed warning about a flood. She initially sought contact with her deceased father, then attributed messages to an “Elder Brother”, the “Guardians” and beings connected with Clarion and another planet called Cerus. Sananda, whom Martin understood as the present identity of Jesus, became an especially important source within the evolving system. This mixture of Christian [revelation]({{ 'revelation/' | relative_url }}), spiritual hierarchy and space travel placed the messages within the broader contactee culture of the early flying-saucer era rather than within conventional astronomical inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

Clarion itself was not a name unique to Martin’s writings. Contactee Truman Bethurum had already claimed encounters with occupants of a spacecraft from a planet called Clarion, publicising the story before and in his 1954 book *Aboard a Flying Saucer*. Accounts of Bethurum’s story described Clarion as hidden from terrestrial observation, a convenient feature for a world that could not be located by ordinary astronomy. The prior circulation of the name does not prove direct borrowing by Martin, but it means that “Clarion” was already part of contemporary UFO folklore rather than a detail known only through her private communications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/bethurum-truman" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bethurum truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bethurum initially told his story at the flying saucer convention at Giant Rock in 1952.... Different entities appeared to speak through Martin, while at least one other participant reportedly claimed to receive spoken communications from space beings. Agreement within that setting could establish consistency for believers without establishing an extraterrestrial source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

## From written messages to a dated prophecy

The decisive shift occurred when the communications moved beyond broad spiritual teaching and supplied a deadline. Martin said a flood would devastate extensive regions before dawn on 21 December 1954. The warning became publicly identifiable through a newspaper story whose headline presented it as a “prophecy from planet Clarion”. Contemporary summaries reported that Martin denied authorship in the ordinary sense, describing the forecast as the substance of numerous automatic-writing messages sent by superior beings who travelled in flying saucers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theparodies.substack.com/p/looking-for-heaven" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theparodies.substack.com">[The Parodies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theparodies.substack.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Parodies Looking for Heaven</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">M. This transformation from private writing to public prophecy occurred through several stages:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **The messages acquired named authorities.** Figures such as Elder Brother, the Guardians and Sananda supplied an otherworldly hierarchy behind Martin’s claims.
* **General spiritual ideas became operational instructions.** Followers were told that a catastrophe was approaching and that certain people could be saved.
* **A date made the claim testable.** The morning of 21 December created a point at which observers could ask whether the flood and rescue had happened.
* **Intermediaries circulated the material.** Charles and Lillian Laughead helped distribute Martin’s messages, while Charles issued press releases warning of the coming event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2025-kelly.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[Gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span></span></span>

The message stream continued to shape expectations as the deadline approached. According to the researchers’ account, the group expected an extraterrestrial visitor around midnight on 20 December to escort them to a spacecraft. Members removed metal objects from their clothing because they believed such items could interfere with boarding. When midnight passed without a visitor, attention shifted to clocks, possible delays and further communications rather than immediately to abandonment of the prediction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

Automatic writing was crucial at this stage because it provided a rapid means of updating the narrative. A delayed rescue did not require contact with a visible craft, a radio signal or an identifiable outside informant. Another sheet of writing could supply a new instruction or explanation while retaining the authority of the same inaccessible source.

The best-known example came in the early hours of 21 December, after neither the rescuers nor the flood had appeared. Martin produced another message stating, in substance, that the group’s spiritual light had persuaded God to spare the world. The non-occurrence of the predicted catastrophe was thus interpreted not as evidence that the warning was false, but as the result of divine intervention prompted by the believers themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2025-kelly.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[Gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span></span></span>

This was an especially powerful reversal because it absorbed both failed elements of the forecast. No flood was visible because the disaster had supposedly been cancelled; no saucer rescue was necessary because the danger had been removed. The same communication method that created the deadline therefore also created the explanation for its failure.

## What the surviving record can establish

The Clarion case contains more documentary material than many vague claims of alien contact. Researchers observed Martin writing, collected or copied some of the resulting texts and recorded the group’s discussions. Newspaper coverage published the basic flood forecast before the deadline. These records make it reasonable to conclude that Martin circulated a prediction in advance and that the expected flood and rescue did not occur.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

The record also helps distinguish several evidential questions that are sometimes collapsed into one:

**Were messages written?** Yes. Multiple accounts describe automatic-writing documents attributed to Martin, and investigators reportedly preserved examples or copies.

**Did Martin and her associates treat them as communications from superior beings?** The contemporary behaviour and published statements strongly indicate that they did.

**Did the messages include an observable forecast?** Yes. A flood and extraterrestrial rescue were expected within a defined period.

**Was the alleged sender independently identified?** No. There was no verified signal, physical visitor, astronomical location or communication channel connecting the writing to Clarion.

**Did the forecast succeed?** No flood or rescue occurred by the stated deadline.

That distinction is central to the case. The documents are evidence for the history of a belief and for what participants expected. They are not evidence that the asserted extraterrestrial correspondents existed.

## Problems with independently checking the source

The Clarion messages were difficult to test at the point of origin because all meaningful verification passed through Martin or closely associated believers. The purported senders could not be questioned by an independent observer. Clarion could not be located. No transmission mechanism was demonstrated. There was no stable identifier by which one message could be authenticated as coming from the same intelligence as another.

Even the language of the communications created room for continual adaptation. Different spiritual beings could issue instructions, revise expectations or elevate the explanation to divine authority. A contradiction could therefore be treated as a change in celestial plans rather than a reason to doubt the communication process.

The method also lacked controls that might distinguish external authorship from ordinary psychological processes. A stronger test would have required information that Martin could not reasonably know, recorded before verification and checked by neutral investigators. Repeatable communications under controlled conditions, consistent answers to blinded questions or independently received messages containing the same unexpected details would also have raised the evidential standard. The surviving accounts do not show such tests.

Instead, apparent confirmation largely occurred inside a receptive social network. Participants shared ideas from occult religion, Christianity and flying-saucer literature; some had encountered related teachings before meeting Martin. Under those circumstances, matching themes are less persuasive than they would be between genuinely isolated witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2025-kelly.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[Gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span></span></span>

There is also a historical complication: the most famous narrative of the group came from covert participant observation. Festinger, Henry Riecken, Stanley Schachter and paid assistants entered the circle while concealing their research purpose. Their presence, combined with intense newspaper interest, makes the surviving narrative less like an untouched record of private revelation and more like an observed—and potentially influenced—social episode.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Prophecy_Fails" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Prophecy Fails</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Prophecy Fails</span></span></span>

Thomas Kelly’s archival reappraisal raises a still more serious concern. Kelly argues that the researchers understated Martin’s pre-deadline publicity, that she and the Laugheads had already distributed messages and sought audiences, and that members of the research team sometimes intervened rather than merely observing. His article also alleges that investigators generated purported psychic communications of their own. These claims do not turn Martin’s failed prophecy into a successful one, but they do weaken confidence in every dramatic detail preserved solely through the classic study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2025-kelly.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[Gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunking “When Prophecy Fails”</span></span></span>

## The deadline is clearer than the messenger

The strongest conclusion is narrower than either believers or later psychological retellings sometimes suggested. Martin produced writings that she attributed to beings associated with Clarion. Those writings, circulated with assistance from her associates, generated a public and dated prediction of flood and rescue. The promised events did not occur.

Beyond that, caution is necessary. The evidence does not independently connect any document to an extraterrestrial source. The name Clarion already circulated in contactee literature. The messages arose within a fluid belief system whose supernatural speakers and instructions could change. After disconfirmation, further writing supplied a reason that converted failure into supposed deliverance.

For the study of failed UFO predictions, the Clarion material is valuable precisely because it preserves the mechanism by which a claim became a deadline. Automatic writing gave an inaccessible source a visible voice, allowed private revelation to be copied and publicised, and enabled the prediction to be revised without obtaining new evidence from the outside world. The pages are therefore a concrete record of prophecy-making—but not verification of the world from which they were said to come.

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
