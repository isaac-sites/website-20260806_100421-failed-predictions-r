## Introduction

A citation loop occurs when multiple articles, videos, forum posts or social media accounts appear to confirm a UFO claim but ultimately trace back to the same original source. The result is an illusion of corroboration: readers encounter many references and mistake quantity for independent evidence. This matters particularly for failed UFO predictions because, after the predicted event does not occur, the online record can still make the forecast appear well documented and widely supported. The real question is not how many websites mention a prediction, but how many independent sources actually gathered original evidence. In many cases, the answer is one—or none beyond the original claimant. ## How circular citation chains form

Citation loops usually develop gradually rather than through deliberate deception. A prediction may first appear in a personal blog, channelled message, interview or podcast. A forum summarises it without adding evidence. A YouTube creator cites the forum discussion. A news aggregation site reports that "online sources" are discussing the prediction, linking to the video rather than the original statement. Later writers cite the aggregation site as though it represents a separate investigation.

At each stage, the number of publications grows while the amount of original evidence remains unchanged. Because every new item looks like another source, readers can easily conclude that multiple independent observers have confirmed the same prediction.

This process is sometimes described as **source laundering**: information acquires an appearance of credibility simply by passing through multiple publications. The wording may change, speculative details may be added, and links to earlier articles may replace links to the original claim. These changes make the chain harder to recognise while doing nothing to strengthen the underlying evidence.

For failed UFO forecasts, the loop can continue even after the predicted date passes. Retrospectives often cite previous articles that were themselves based entirely on the original prediction, giving the impression that the forecast had substantial documentary support all along.

## Clues that several sources share one origin

One of the easiest ways to test apparent corroboration is to work backwards instead of forwards. Independent reporting should branch outward towards multiple witnesses, documents or datasets. Citation loops converge towards a single point.

Common warning signs include:

* Several articles repeating distinctive phrases or unusual quotations almost word for word.
* Different websites citing one another instead of linking to primary documents.
* References to "multiple reports" where every report ultimately cites the same interview, blog or forum thread.
* New publications introducing no new witnesses, photographs, documents or observational data.
* Broken citation chains in which later writers omit the original source entirely.

Another clue is identical uncertainty. If every article repeats the same gaps—no location verification, no additional witnesses, no physical evidence and no contemporaneous documentation—it is unlikely they represent independent investigations.

The UK House of Commons Library's guidance on fact-checking recommends tracing claims back to their original source and examining the original context precisely because repetition alone cannot establish accuracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10820/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commonslibrary.parliament.uk">[House of Commons Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commonslibrary.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">House of Commons Library How to check facts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">House of Commons LibraryHow to check facts - House of Commons Library...</span></span></span>

## Why failed UFO predictions are especially vulnerable

Predictions create an unusual information environment because evidence is largely absent before the forecasted event. During that period, discussion itself becomes the main source of content.

A prediction of a specific UFO appearance might generate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-stack" markdown="1">

* reaction videos,
* podcasts,
* forum debates,
* blog summaries,
* news articles about the online discussion,
* social media reposts,
* later retrospective articles.

</div>

To someone searching months later, dozens of search results may appear to indicate extensive documentation. In reality, every item may ultimately depend on the same unsupported prediction.

This distinction is critical when assessing failed forecasts. Numerous references can demonstrate that many people discussed the claim, but they cannot demonstrate that anyone independently confirmed it before, during or after the predicted event.

## Official investigations have identified circular reporting

The idea that repeated stories can reinforce one another without independent evidence is not unique to UFO sceptics. In its historical review of UAP claims, the US Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) concluded that many longstanding allegations about hidden UFO programmes resulted from **circular reporting**, in which a relatively small group repeatedly passed along inaccurate claims over many years. Officials emphasised that this process often occurred without intentional deception; sincere repetition was enough to create the appearance of corroboration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3701297/dod-report-discounts-sightings-of-extraterrestrial-technology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDOD Report Discounts Sightings of Extraterrestrial Technology &gt; U.S. Department of Defense &gt; Defense Department New...</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3701297/dod-report-discounts-sightings-of-extraterrestrial-technology/)

Although AARO's assessment concerns historical government programme claims rather than failed public predictions specifically, the underlying mechanism is the same. When sources continually reference one another instead of producing independent evidence, the apparent strength of a claim can grow even while its evidential basis remains unchanged.

## Breaking the loop to test the claim

Readers can often dismantle a citation loop by asking a small number of practical questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Where did this claim first appear?** Identify the earliest available publication rather than the most widely shared one.
2. **Does each later source contribute new evidence?** New witnesses, documents, measurements or observations matter; rewritten summaries do not.
3. **Would the claim survive if every secondary article disappeared?** If removing copied material leaves only a single unsupported statement, the apparent corroboration was illusory.
4. **Did any independent observers document the predicted event?** For failed UFO forecasts, this is usually the decisive question.

</div>

This approach shifts attention from the volume of discussion to the quality and independence of the underlying evidence.

## What citation loops can and cannot show

Citation loops are evidence that information spread successfully—not that it became more accurate. They can demonstrate public interest, media attention or the popularity of a prediction, but they cannot convert repeated assertions into independent confirmation.

When evaluating failed UFO predictions, the most reliable indicator is not how many websites discuss the forecast but whether separate investigators, using independent evidence, reached the same conclusion without relying on one another. If every path leads back to the same unsupported origin, the apparent consensus is a product of repetition rather than verification.

    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
