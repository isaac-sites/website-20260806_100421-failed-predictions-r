## Introduction

A UFO prediction is only useful for later evaluation if the evidence survives in a form that independent readers can inspect. A compact evidence package is a small, self-contained case file that preserves not only the prediction itself but also the information needed to verify exactly what was claimed, when it was published, and whether it was altered [before or after]({{ 'before-or-after/' | relative_url }}) the stated deadline. The goal is not to prove that a prediction is true or false in advance. Instead, it is to ensure that any later judgement rests on authentic, traceable records rather than memory, paraphrase or retrospective editing.

For research into failed UFO predictions, a well-constructed evidence package allows different investigators to reach the same factual starting point. It reduces disputes over wording, dates and provenance while making the prediction straightforward to test against the promised outcome.

## The Minimum Files and Metadata to Collect

A compact case file should contain only the material needed to reconstruct the claim and verify its authenticity. Keeping the package focused makes it easier for other researchers to review and preserve.

The essential contents are:

* **Original source** – the webpage, document, email, video transcript, social media post or printed publication containing the prediction.

* **Readable preservation copy** – a PDF or screenshot showing how the prediction appeared at the time it was collected.
* **Archive copy** – an independently archived version, such as a Wayback Machine capture or another trusted web archive, demonstrating that the material existed before the prediction deadline. Independent archives provide stronger evidence than locally stored screenshots alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://support.archive-it.org/hc/en-us/articles/208333963-About-Wayback-URLs-and-messages" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: support.archive-it.org">[Archive-It]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">support.archive-it.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">About Wayback URLs and messages – Archive-It Help Center</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">About Wayback URLs and messages – Archive-It Help CenterJuly 17, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 17, 2026</span></span></span>

* **Publication metadata** – author, publisher, URL, publication date, access date and any visible timestamps.
* **Supporting files** – linked images, downloadable documents or referenced attachments if they are necessary to understand the prediction.
* **Verification notes** – a brief explanation describing where the material was obtained and whether any parts were unavailable or already deleted.

If the source exists in several places, preserving more than one independent copy is preferable. A claimant's own website may later change, while an archived copy or contemporary news report can demonstrate how the prediction originally appeared.

## Creating a Structured Claim Sheet

The centrepiece of the package should be a single claim sheet that separates the prediction's observable commitments from surrounding commentary.

A practical claim sheet should record:

FieldPurposeClaim identifierUnique reference for the caseClaimantPerson or organisation making the predictionOriginal sourceComplete citation and URLDate publishedEarliest verifiable publication dateDeadlineWhen the prediction should be assessedExact wordingDirect quotation of the predictionObservable eventWhat should happen if the prediction succeedsLocationGeographic scope, if specifiedExpected witnessesPublic, selected individuals or private recipientsPromised evidenceLanding, lights, photographs, broadcasts, official [announcement]({{ 'two-dates/' | relative_url }}) or other observable outcomeConditionsAny qualifications, exceptions or contingency clausesAssessment statusPending, fulfilled, failed or indeterminate after the deadline

Separating these fields discourages later reinterpretation. For example, a prediction promising that "a spacecraft will remain visible worldwide for three days beginning on 14 October" is materially different from one suggesting that spiritually receptive people "may notice unusual lights". Recording the exact language prevents the stronger claim from being softened after the deadline.

Where interpretation is unavoidable, distinguish clearly between **quoted text** and **researcher summary**.

## Preserve Proof of Timing and Authenticity

The value of an evidence package depends on demonstrating that the material existed before the predicted event.

Useful timing evidence includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* archived webpages with preserved capture dates;
* dated printed publications;
* newspaper reports quoting the prediction before the deadline;
* broadcast recordings with identifiable transmission dates;
* mailing-list archives or newsletters distributed before the event;
* digital files carrying reliable publication metadata.

</div>

When preserving online material, it is good practice to record:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the live URL;
* archived URL;
* capture date;
* retrieval date;
* file size;
* file format.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/fdd/fdd000235" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[loc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AR C_IA, Internet Archive ARC file format</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ARC_IA, Internet Archive ARC file formatApril 29, 2024 — ARC_IA, INTERNET ARCHIVE ARC FILE FORMAT &gt;&gt; Back Table of Contents * Identificat...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 29, 2024</span></span></span>

</div>

## Include Integrity Checks Without Overcomplicating the File

The package should be simple enough for ordinary researchers to inspect while still providing evidence that locally stored files have not changed.

Useful integrity measures include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* recording SHA-256 hashes for downloaded files;
* keeping original filenames;
* avoiding unnecessary editing or recompression;
* documenting any conversions, such as HTML to PDF;
* storing original files separately from annotated copies.

</div>

Hash values are valuable for verifying local files over time, but archived webpages present additional challenges because replayed archived pages may change in technically insignificant ways, such as embedded resources or archive-generated elements. Researchers therefore should not rely on webpage hashes alone as proof that archived content is unchanged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10256179/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMed Central (PMC)Hashes are not suitable to verify fixity of the public archived web - PMCJune 9, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 9, 2023</span></span></span>

## Keep Primary Evidence Separate from Analysis

One of the most common weaknesses in UFO prediction case files is mixing evidence with conclusions.

A cleaner structure separates three layers:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Primary evidence** – original documents, archived pages and recordings.
2. **Extraction** – the structured claim sheet summarising observable commitments.
3. **Assessment** – later evaluation after the prediction deadline.

</div>

Keeping these layers distinct allows future researchers to disagree with the interpretation without disputing the preserved evidence itself.

If additional commentary is added later—for example, explanations offered after a failed prediction—it should be placed in a separate section rather than inserted into the original record.

A compact package should be understandable without requiring access to the collector's computer or notes.

A practical folder structure might contain:

* **01_Original_Source**
* **02_Archive_Copies**

Each file should have descriptive names rather than generic labels such as "image1.png". Including dates in ISO 8601 format (YYYY-MM-DD) makes chronological sorting straightforward.

A short readme can explain:

* what the package contains;
* when it was assembled;
* which files are originals;
* any known limitations, such as missing attachments or inaccessible pages.

This organisation enables another researcher to inspect the prediction quickly without reconstructing the collection process from scratch.

## Common Mistakes That Weaken a Case File

Even when the underlying prediction is well documented, several avoidable errors reduce evidential value:

* preserving only screenshots while omitting the original source URL;
* quoting excerpts instead of recording the complete prediction;
* failing to preserve publication dates;
* mixing later explanations with the original prediction;
* relying on a single copy when multiple independent archives are available;
* editing files without retaining the originals;
* omitting metadata describing where and when files were obtained.

These shortcomings make later disputes harder to resolve because reviewers cannot independently verify the evidence or distinguish original material from later interpretation.

## Why a Compact Package Matters

Most disputes over failed UFO predictions do not arise because the predicted event is difficult to observe. They arise because the documentary record becomes incomplete, edited or fragmented after the deadline has passed. A compact evidence package addresses that problem by preserving a small but complete collection of source material, metadata and integrity information. When assembled before the prediction's deadline, it allows later assessments to focus on whether the predicted event occurred rather than on reconstructing what was originally claimed.
