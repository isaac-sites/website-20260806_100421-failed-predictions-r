## Introduction

Many UFO or unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP) reports remain unresolved for a simple reason: investigators do not have enough reliable information to determine what was observed. An unresolved case is therefore a statement about the quality of the available evidence, not about the likelihood of an extraterrestrial explanation. This distinction is especially important when discussing [failed predictions relating to UFOs]({{ 'failed-predictions-relating-to-ufos/' | relative_url }}). A report that cannot be identified does not provide evidence that a separate prediction about alien contact, disclosure or future events was correct.

Modern official investigations consistently reach the same conclusion. Missing metadata, uncertain distance, incomplete sensor records and a lack of independent corroboration often prevent confident identification. These same investigations also state that [unresolved cases]({{ 'unresolved-cases/' | relative_url }}) should not be interpreted as positive evidence for alien technology. Instead, they remain open because the available data are insufficient to test either ordinary or extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smd-cms.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smd-cms.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smd-cms.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science NASA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

## How Distance and Sensor Limits Distort Interpretation

One of the largest obstacles in UFO analysis is that cameras record angular position rather than true distance. Without knowing how far away an object is, almost every other estimate becomes uncertain.

A small nearby object and a large distant object can appear identical in a photograph. Likewise, a slow-moving balloon several kilometres away may produce image motion resembling a fast-moving aircraft if the observer is also moving.

### Why Unknown Range Matters

Distance affects calculations of:

* apparent speed
* altitude
* size
* acceleration
* manoeuvrability

If the object's range is unknown, many different physical situations can produce the same image.

For this reason, investigators generally avoid calculating extraordinary performance from a single camera view unless range can be independently established.

### Camera Motion Can Create Illusions

Airborne videos introduce another complication: the observing aircraft is itself moving.

Parallax—the apparent movement of an object against a background caused by observer motion—can make distant objects appear to race across the landscape even when their actual motion is modest. To distinguish genuine target movement from camera geometry, analysts require information such as aircraft position, velocity, viewing angle, field of view and target range. Without these measurements, dramatic-looking motion may simply reflect perspective rather than exceptional flight characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufouap.net/en/explainers/parallax-in-uap-videos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufouap.net">[UFO Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufouap.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Archive Parallax in UAP Videos: Why Objects Seem Fast &#124; Global UFO Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ArchiveParallax in UAP Videos: Why Objects Seem Fast &#124; Global UFO ArchiveJuly 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Corroboration Matters More Than a Single Witness

Eyewitness testimony can be sincere and detailed while still leaving important questions unanswered.

Human observers generally cannot estimate distance, altitude or speed accurately when viewing unfamiliar aerial objects against an empty sky. Night-time observations are particularly vulnerable because there are few visual references.

Independent corroboration greatly improves confidence. Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* radar matching the visual observation
* another camera from a different location
* infrared and visible recordings captured simultaneously
* satellite or astronomical records
* air traffic data
* weather observations

</div>

When these independent records are unavailable or inconsistent, investigators often cannot distinguish between competing explanations.

This does not imply that witnesses are dishonest. Rather, it reflects the limits of human perception and the need for multiple independent measurements in scientific investigation. ## Why an Open Case Is Not Positive Alien Evidence

One common misunderstanding is to treat an unresolved case as indirect evidence for extraterrestrial technology.

This is not how scientific reasoning works.

An unresolved classification simply means investigators cannot presently determine which explanation best fits the available evidence. Several ordinary explanations may remain plausible without any one of them being confirmed.

Official investigations repeatedly emphasise this distinction. The US Department of Defense and the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) report that many cases remain in an [active archive]({{ 'active-archive/' | relative_url }}) because they lack sufficient scientific or sensor data. Those cases may later be resolved if additional information becomes available. At the same time, AARO states that it has found no verifiable evidence of extraterrestrial beings, activity or technology in the cases it has analysed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

Logically, unresolved evidence supports neither an ordinary explanation nor an extraordinary one until additional data narrow the possibilities.

## Better Data Often Changes Earlier Conclusions

Many cases initially considered mysterious have later been explained once additional information became available.

Improved analysis can include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* better satellite tracking
* more complete aircraft records
* weather archives
* improved image reconstruction
* sensor calibration
* higher-quality original recordings

</div>

Recent AARO reporting illustrates this process. New analytical capabilities enabled investigators to resolve large numbers of previously unexplained reports by identifying satellite flares and other ordinary causes that earlier analyses could not confidently establish. The limiting factor was not necessarily the observation itself but the quality and completeness of supporting data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>(https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3965403/dod-examining-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena/)

This demonstrates that an unresolved case is not permanently mysterious. It is often a temporary consequence of incomplete information.

## Why This Matters When Assessing Failed UFO Predictions

Within the broader discussion of failed UFO predictions, unresolved sightings should not be used to rescue forecasts that never came true.

A prediction claims that a future event will occur. It succeeds or fails according to whether that event happens.

An unresolved UFO report concerns a past observation that investigators cannot presently identify. Its uncertainty does not increase the probability that unrelated predictions about alien arrivals, government disclosure or other future events were correct.

The [two questions]({{ 'two-questions/' | relative_url }}) rely on different standards of evidence. One concerns incomplete observation; the other concerns whether a forecast accurately described future reality. Confusing these categories allows uncertainty about one event to be misused as apparent support for an entirely different claim.

Understanding why some UFO cases remain unresolved therefore clarifies an important point: insufficient data justify continued investigation, but they do not constitute positive evidence for extraterrestrial explanations or validate predictions that failed to materialise.
